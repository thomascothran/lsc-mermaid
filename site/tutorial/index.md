A Live Sequence Chart is a picture of people, or machines, passing messages to each other.
It can tell a story ("here is one way an order at a café can go") or state a rule ("whenever
someone pays, they must get their drink"). This tutorial teaches you to write them, one small
idea at a time.

You don't need to know anything about programming, diagrams or the notations they grow out of.
Every chart in the tutorial is drawn live from the text under it. Change the text and the
picture changes with it. Each lesson ends with something to try and a question to check your
understanding, and each has a model answer you can open when you are ready.

The same story runs through the whole tutorial: a customer orders a coffee from a barista, and
pays at the till. It grows a little in every lesson. The last part reads a chart from the paper
that introduced Live Sequence Charts, Damm and Harel's "LSCs: Breathing Life into Message
Sequence Charts" (2001).
